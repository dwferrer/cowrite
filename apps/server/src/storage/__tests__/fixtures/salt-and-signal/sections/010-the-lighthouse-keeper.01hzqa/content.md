Mara Voss climbed the hundred and nine steps of the Cinder Point light with the storm
ledger under her arm. The lamp room smelled of brass polish and old rain. From the
gallery she could count every hull in the harbor: four trawlers, the mail packet, and
the harrow-boat that should have sailed on Tuesday.

She logged the glass at falling, the wind at north-by-east, and the sea the color of
unpolished pewter. Eleven years of entries in the same careful hand, and not one of
them admitted what she listened for on nights like this: the voices that ride in on
the fog, patient as the tide, asking to be counted too.
