The storm glass stood on the quayside office shelf, a sealed teardrop of brine and
camphor that the old wreckers had trusted more than any almanac. All morning the
crystals inside it had been climbing, feathering upward like frost on a window that
faces open water.

Mara set it on the counter between herself and the clerk. Neither of them spoke. The
glass ticked once against the wood, the way it did when the pressure dropped fast, and
the clerk slid the mooring ledger across without being asked.
